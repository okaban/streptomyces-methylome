# One recognition sequence carries 4mC and 6mA on the same DNA strand in *Streptomyces coelicolor*

=== L19 (1647 chars)
Bacteria methylate their own genomes, and the marks are not inert: they time replication, discriminate self from non-self, and in some species switch gene expression. Which bases are marked, and how fully, is set by the methyltransferases a genome encodes. One actinomycete methylome has been measured directly: SMRT sequencing placed 4mC at two recognition sequences in this genome. That work left open whether the cytosine mark is 4mC rather than 5mC, whether the genome carries 6mA, and how either relates to transcription. To our knowledge no bacterial methylome has been measured as position-level occupancy alongside a transcriptome from the same cultures. Here we show that the *Streptomyces coelicolor* M145 genome carries 4mC and N6-methyladenine (6mA) but no detectable 5-methylcytosine (5mC), at two recognition sequences, one of which carries both chemistries on the same DNA strand. Of 572 positions previously tabulated as 5mC, 85.0% fall within the sequence space of these two sequences. 4mC occupies the non-palindromic 8-mer TGGCCGGC at 1,886 of 2,093 positions (90.1%), and 6mA the first adenine of AAGCCCG at 24.2% of its 1,334 instances. Reads spanning both AAGCCCG positions place the two marks on one molecule more often than chance, a configuration unresolved in any dual-specificity system with a known strand arrangement. Occupancy was unchanged across three metabolic stages and showed no resolvable transcript-level association; 5′ context and host-gene orientation predict where the adenine mark occurs instead. In liquid culture this methylome tracks sequence rather than transcription: organised, but not instructive.

=== L35 (779 chars)
DNA methylation is among the most ancient regulatory modifications in biology, yet its functional role in bacteria remains poorly defined. In *Escherichia coli*, N6-methyladenine (6mA) at GATC motifs coordinates DNA replication timing, mismatch repair, and virulence gene expression [@Marinus2009Roles]. Bacterial genomes carry 6mA, 4mC and 5mC, and these three chemistries differ in genomic distribution and developmental dynamics [@Beaulaurier2018Deciphering]. Methylomes and transcriptomes have been read from the same bacterial cultures, including in *Streptomyces* across two growth phases [@Fang2022Methylation], but we are not aware of a study reporting occupancy at the individual positions of a recognition sequence, which is the level at which a methyltransferase acts.

=== L39 (594 chars)
Bisulfite sequencing cannot distinguish 5mC from 4mC: it reads methylation as protection from bisulfite-mediated C→U deamination, and both the 5-methyl and the N4-methyl group impede that deamination, giving identical unconverted-C readouts. Nanopore sequencing of native DNA resolves the distinct ionic-current signatures of 4mC, 5mC and unmodified cytosine, so a cytosine mark can be assigned to a chemistry at base resolution without chemical conversion. The one genome-wide methylome of this strain, by PacBio SMRT, reported two 4mC recognition sequences and no 6mA [@Lutz2019Architecture].

=== L41 (533 chars)
Here we integrated nanopore 4mC/6mA methylome profiling with RNA-seq at three sampling times in *S. coelicolor* M145 (T1, 12 h; T2, 24 h; T3, 50 h; three biological replicates each), spanning vegetative growth, the metabolic transition and the producing phase — an interval over which submerged cultures pass through a described sequence of mycelial differentiation and programmed cell death [@Manteca2008Mycelium; @Rioseras2014Mycelium]. The timepoints distinguished biomass accumulation and the onset of secondary metabolism (§R1).

=== L63 (367 chars)
Because bisulfite chemistry cannot separate 5mC from 4mC, the cytosine mark in this genome had never been assigned. We therefore read both cytosine channels directly from native DNA before interpreting any site. Both cytosine channels and the adenine channel were called from the same reads, so 4mC, 5mC and 6mA were all available to be detected (Methods; Figure 2d).

Finding both chemistries in one short motif raised a question a pileup cannot answer: are the two marks on the same strand, and do they occur on the same molecule? On the same strand of a motif instance, 475 pairs carry both 4mC (position 5) and 6mA (position 1 or 2), spanning 401 of 1,334 instances, versus none of 1,693,612 same-strand A/C pairs outside the recognition sequence (Supplementary Table S3a); 38.58% of HAAGCCCG instances carry both marks at T1.

Against every catalogued dual-specificity system the configuration is unreported. Of the 51 dual 4mC/6mA entries in REBASE v602 [@Roberts2014Rebase], 50 place the marks on opposite strands and the remaining entry leaves one position undetermined (Figure 3c; Supplementary Table S9c); the same separation holds in the described cross-talk and shared-target cases [@Morgan2016Novel; @Galeone2025Decoding; @Shi2024Crosstalk; @FurmanekBlaszk2009Mboii]. Four of the 51 are actinobacterial, so we claim no first for actinobacteria — only that the same-strand arrangement, and its demonstration on single molecules, is not catalogued there.

We next asked what predicts which instances carry a mark. The 6mA mark tracks host-gene orientation: occupancy at AAGCCCG is 34.89% on the coding strand versus 10.04% on the template strand at T1 (*n* = 708 and 448; odds ratio 4.80, 95% CI 3.40–6.77), with the same contrast at T2 and T3 (Supplementary Table S4b). Adjustment does not remove it (odds ratio 2.89 at T1, 2.96 at T2, 4.18 at T3; Supplementary Table S4c). 4mC at position 5 shows no difference (1.10, 95% CI 0.83–1.46), nor does 4mC at TGGCCGGC (0.84, 0.60–1.18), with power to exclude a 6mA-sized effect. === L245 (1024 chars)
The promoter-proximal deficit in 4mC site density is largely accounted for by promoter architecture rather than by the methylome; the smaller surviving 6mA level deficit is treated above. σ-dependent recognition requires an AT-containing −10 element and open-complex strand separation is cheapest in AT-rich DNA, creating an AT island in a GC-rich genome [@DiSalvo2018Promfinder; @Jeong2016Dynamic]; TSS-resolved transcriptomes recover an AT-containing TANNNT-type −10 hexamer [@Jeong2016Dynamic; @Lee2022Genome], reproduced here across start sites (Supplementary Figure 7a). A recognition sequence of 87.5% GC is therefore disfavoured at the transcription start without further mechanism: TGGCCGGC thins there, while AAGCCCG (71.4% GC, the genome average) shows no systematic dip (Supplementary Figure 7b). About one third remains unexplained — against windows of identical GC count TGGCCGGC stays depleted at the core — and dinucleotide structure within the −10 element and translation-start constraints remain unmodelled.

=== L257 (1258 chars)
Neither chemistry is yet assigned to an enzyme, and the candidate sets narrow unequally. On the adenine side five genes carry a motif IV within a DNA amino-methyltransferase domain; narrowing them to three relies on a size heuristic, and the largest survivor carries HPPF in place of the canonical DPPY, a variant unassignable to either chemistry on domain evidence. Both genes the floor removes sit below the genome-wide median at every timepoint, so it is not separating transcribed from silent genes (Supplementary Figure 8). No candidate can be assigned to 4mC at either position, because amino-methyltransferase families and motif IV are shared by N4-cytosine and N6-adenine enzymes and no Pfam family is specific to 4mC; a type III Mod route is the next place to look. Lutz *et al.* reached the opposite assignment from the same families, calling SCO3104, SCO3510 and SCO6885 putative N4mC methyltransferases [@Lutz2019Architecture]. The difference is not in the evidence but in what was measurable: with no 6mA detected, N4mC was the only chemistry left to assign. Our detection of 6mA at these motifs removes that constraint without resolving the assignment, and our own narrowing excludes two of the three at the size floor (Supplementary Figure 5).

=== L269 (802 chars)
Two methylation activities operate in *S. coelicolor* M145, one modifying a single short motif twice on the same strand and, by single-molecule data, on the same DNA molecule; this configuration is not catalogued among the 51 dual 4mC/6mA systems in REBASE v602. Neither activity is associated with transcript level at the effect sizes this design resolves, and the methylome is unchanged as secondary metabolism begins, so no methylation switch accompanies that transition in these cultures. No established function is supported; what the data predict is where the adenine mark occurs, from its 5′ context and host-gene orientation, while the cytosine methyltransferase remains unassigned within a search space this work defines. The methylome is organised by sequence and silent toward transcription.
